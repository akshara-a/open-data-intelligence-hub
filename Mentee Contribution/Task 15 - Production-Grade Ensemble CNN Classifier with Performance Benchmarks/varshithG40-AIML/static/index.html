Theoretical Answers & Complete Project Guide

Comprehensive explanations addressing all 12 questions from Section 72 with empirical evidence from the benchmark suite.

Q1: What is an ensemble?
An ensemble is a machine learning methodology where multiple independently trained models are strategically combined to generate a single unified prediction. By aggregating diverse hypothesis spaces, ensembles reduce variance and produce more resilient decisions.
Q2: Why is this system called an Ensemble CNN Classifier?
Because the underlying feature extractors are multiple Convolutional Neural Network architectures (CNN 1 Baseline, CNN 2 Regularized, CNN 3 Deeper), and their individual classification probability outputs are integrated through an ensembling meta-function.
Q3: Why might three CNN models perform better together than one CNN?
Different CNN architectures explore distinct loss landscape trajectories. When one model makes an error due to local overfitting or noise, the other two models can outvote or out-weigh the error, correcting the final prediction.
Q4: Why should the CNN models be different from each other?
Model diversity is critical. If all three models are identical, they will learn identical features and fail on the exact same corner cases. Having varied architectures (e.g. standard conv vs Batch Normalization/Dropout vs Global Average Pooling) provides orthogonal representations.
Q5: What is the difference between Majority Voting and Soft Voting?
Majority Voting (Hard Voting) considers only the final discrete predicted class labels and selects the most frequent vote. Soft Voting computes the continuous arithmetic mean of the class probability distributions, enabling highly confident models to appropriately influence the final outcome.
Q6: Which ensemble strategy produced the highest accuracy?
Both Soft Voting and Weighted Soft Voting achieved 100.00% test accuracy on clean images and produced lower log loss (0.0002) than hard voting.
Q7: Did the ensemble outperform every individual CNN?
Yes. The ensemble outperformed CNN 2 (93.33% accuracy) and matched CNN 3 (100.00%) on clean data, while outperforming CNN 3 on cropped distortions (93.33% vs 80.00%).
Q8: What was the accuracy difference between the best CNN and the ensemble?
On clean test images, the difference was 0.00% (both reached 100.00%). Under center cropping distortion, the Ensemble was +13.33 percentage points more accurate than CNN 3.
Q9: What happened to inference latency after introducing the ensemble?
Inference latency increased from 183.63 ms (CNN 3) to 635.05 ms (Sequential Ensemble) and 468.77 ms (Parallel Ensemble), representing a 2.5x–3.4x latency increase.
Q10: What happened to throughput?
Throughput dropped by 70.3%, decreasing from 5.4 images/second for CNN 3 down to 1.6 images/second for sequential ensemble execution.
Q11: Was the ensemble more robust against noisy or modified images?
Yes. In spatial perturbations like cropping and zoom shifts, the ensemble demonstrated higher resilience (93.33%) by filtering out individual CNN 3 mistakes.
Q12: Would you deploy the ensemble in production? Explain your decision using evidence from your benchmarks.
Decision:
  • For Edge / Low-Latency (<200ms): Deploy CNN 3 due to its 183.6 ms latency, 0.86 MB size, and 70k parameter footprint.
  • For Offline Quality Control / Auditing: Deploy the Soft Voting Ensemble to benefit from multi-model consensus verification and automated disagreement gating.
